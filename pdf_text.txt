Abstract—Automated surface quality assurance in high-
throughput food container and packaging manufacturing requires
robust, sub-millimeter flaw localization capable of handling
arbitrary spatial orientations. Conventional machine vision frame-
works predominantly rely on horizontal axis-aligned bounding
boxes (HBB), which encompass substantial extraneous background
pixels when encapsulating slender, diagonally oriented flaws
such as scratches, edge fissures, and material wrinkles. This
background noise causes severe feature dilution, resulting in
prohibitive miss rates for micro-defects. In this paper, we present
an end-to-end industrial robotic inspection pipeline driven by
an optimized YOLOv8 Oriented Bounding Box (YOLOv8-OBB)
deep neural architecture tailored specifically for thermoformed
and injection-molded rice packaging casings. To resolve critical
detection bottlenecks on small-scale anomalies, we eliminate spatial
mosaic augmentation, suppress excessive geometric translation
and scaling, and employ an AdamW optimizer coupled with cosine
learning rate scheduling. The proposed framework is evaluated on
a benchmark dataset comprising 5,065 annotated industrial images
across nine defect categories:black dots,defective foreign bodies,
flange bad cutting,insects,intense scratches,pinholes,scratches,
shrivel wrinkles, andyellow points. Experimental evaluations
demonstrate that the optimized YOLOv8-OBB model improves
the mean Average Precision at IoU 0.50 (mAP50) from an initial
baseline of 49.02% to 97.55%, while elevating the detection rate
of critical micro-scratches from 12.61% to 95.85%. Integrated
with an Open Neural Network Exchange (ONNX) deployment
runtime, the vision system achieves an inference speed of 24.0 ms
per casing, enabling continuous, real-time rejection on conveyor
lines operating at over 120 units per minute.
Index Terms—Surface defect detection, oriented bounding box
(OBB), YOLOv8, robotic vision, industrial packaging inspection,
small object detection, automated optical inspection (AOI), quality
engineering.
I. INTRODUCTION
H
IGH-SPEED industrial manufacturing of food preserva-
tion containers, particularly polymer-based thermoformed
rice casings, mandates rigorous automated quality control to
ensure structural hermeticity, shelf-life integrity, and consumer
safety. In modern high-throughput manufacturing facilities,
packaging casings are produced via rapid injection molding
and thermoforming cycles exceeding thousands of units per
hour. During these intense thermal and mechanical cycles,
packaging containers are subject to diverse surface anomalies
including polymer carbonization, guillotine trimming fissures,
surface abrasion scratches, micro-pinholes, material wrinkling,
and biological contamination [10], [22]. Micro-perforations
spanning less than 0.2mm in diameter and subtle surface
scratches directly breach the protective barrier properties of the
container. Such breaches permit oxygen permeation, microbial
infiltration, and moisture egress, accelerating food spoilage and
precipitating massive commercial product recalls [27].
Historically, quality control in packaging lines has depended
upon human visual inspection or traditional rule-based au-
tomated optical inspection (AOI) architectures [28]. Human
manual sorting suffers from intrinsic limitations: physiological
eye fatigue, cognitive lapses, subjectivity in grading borderline
flaws, and an inability to maintain inspection fidelity at
conveyance velocities exceeding 0.5m/s [29]. Conversely, clas-
sic rule-based computer vision methodologies—relying upon
handcrafted edge filters (Sobel, Canny), spatial morphological
operations, and adaptive thresholding (Otsu)—exhibit brittle
performance under real-world factory dynamics. Injection-
molded polymer casings possess curved geometries, non-
uniform specular reflectivity, and subtle background texture vari-
ations caused by fluctuating mold temperatures. Consequently,
rule-based algorithms suffer from excessive false-positive spikes
whenever ambient factory lighting shifts or minute conveyor
vibrations induce optical shadow artifacts [9], [21].
Over the past three years, convolutional neural networks
(CNNs) and deep single-stage object detection architectures
have fundamentally transformed industrial quality engineer-
ing [2], [11]. Single-stage detectors from the You Only Look
Once (YOLO) lineage have achieved preeminence due to their
favorable Pareto trade-off between inference throughput and
mean Average Precision (mAP) [9], [15]. Nevertheless, conven-
tional object detectors represent candidate detections utilizing
standard Horizontal Bounding Boxes (HBB) parameterized by
four spatial coordinates (xmin,y min,x max,y max). While HBB
formulations remain effective for isotropic, upright objects in
natural scenes, they impose severe physical and mathematical

pathologies when applied to industrial surface metrology:
1) Background Interference and Feature Dilution:Sur-
face flaws on industrial casings frequently exhibit extreme
aspect ratios (>10 : 1 ) and non-orthogonal orientations,
such as diagonal abrasions, cutting fissures, and curved
shrinkage wrinkles. An axis-aligned rectangle bounding a
45◦ diagonal scratch encloses more than 85% undamaged
casing background. During convolutional feature pooling
and region aggregation, background pixels overpower the
localized defect signature, diluting gradient propagation
and causing the model to miss subtle flaw boundaries [3],
[13].
2) High Spatial Overlap and Non-Maximum Suppression
Failure:When multiple micro-defects occur in close
spatial proximity (such as clusters of pinholes or neigh-
boring micro-cracks), their horizontal bounding boxes
severely overlap in the 2D Cartesian coordinate plane.
Standard Non-Maximum Suppression (NMS) algorithms
erroneously discard genuine defect instances because
their horizontal Intersection-over-Union (IoU) exceeds
suppression thresholds, leading to false negatives on
clustered flaws [23], [26].
3) Robotic Inaccuracy in Actuation:High-speed au-
tomated rejection lines require precise spatial angle
coordinates (θ) to guide pneumatic diverters, robotic
pick-and-place end-effectors, and laser marking heads.
Horizontal bounding boxes offer zero orientation infor-
mation, preventing robotic grippers from aligning along
the natural flaw axis [19], [24].
To resolve these fundamental limitations, Oriented Bounding
Box (OBB) detection networks have emerged, parameter-
izing candidate objects as a rotated five-dimensional tuple
(xc,yc,w,h,θ) [1], [8]. However, simply transitioning from an
HBB architecture to an OBB architecture does not automatically
guarantee high industrial detection accuracy. Crucially, standard
deep learning training regimes employ aggressive geometric
augmentations—most notably four-image mosaic stitching and
extensive random scaling—that were designed for macroscopic
natural scene objects (MS COCO dataset) [20]. When applied to
industrial packaging lines containing micro-scale flaws (such
as 10-pixel scratches and 8-pixel pinholes), these standard
augmentations shrink flaw features to sub-pixel noise, causing
severe training failure and resulting in unacceptable recall on
critical defect classes.
In this research, we engineer, validate, and deploy an
end-to-end automated robotic vision system driven by an
optimized YOLOv8 Oriented Bounding Box (YOLOv8-OBB)
deep neural architecture tailored specifically for industrial
rice packaging containers. By identifying and neutralizing the
underlying augmentation pathology and incorporating Gaussian
Wasserstein Distance spatial regression, our framework achieves
exceptional detection fidelity across micro-scale surface defects.
The primary contributions of this paper are organized as
follows:
• We design and implement a comprehensive industrial
robotic vision inspection pipeline integrating high-speed
CMOS telecentric image acquisition, coaxial diffuse dome
illumination, edge GPU acceleration, and deterministic
pneumatic rejection actuation.
• We formulate an orientation-aware spatial loss framework
incorporating Probabilistic Gaussian Wasserstein Distance
(GWD) loss and continuous periodic angular regulariza-
tion, eliminating boundary discontinuity flip artifacts on
arbitrary-oriented surface flaws.
• We uncover a critical training pathology in industrial
small-defect learning, demonstrating that standard mosaic
augmentation and aggressive affine scaling systematically
destroy micro-defect features. We formulate an augmen-
tation mitigation protocol that elevates micro-scratch
detection accuracy from a failing 12.61% baseline to
95.85%.
• We conduct exhaustive validation across 5,065 production-
grade casing images encompassing nine distinct industrial
defect categories, achieving an overall mAP50 of 97.55%
and an edge-optimized ONNX runtime latency of 24.0 ms
(41.6 FPS).
• We provide a thorough analysis of operational challenges,
physical illumination constraints, hardware memory bot-
tlenecks, and comparative cross-architecture benchmarks.

II. COMPREHENSIVERELATEDWORK
Recent academic and industrial investigations at the in-
tersection of deep learning and automated manufacturing
quality control can be categorized into four primary domains:
vision-based surface defect detection, oriented bounding box
architectures, micro-flaw localization challenges, and robotic
optical sorting.
A. Deep Learning for Industrial Surface Defect Inspection
Between 2023 and 2026, deep convolutional neural net-
works have progressively superseded dual-stage region-proposal
networks (such as Faster R-CNN) in factory manufacturing
environments due to stringent millisecond-level cycle time
requirements [2], [28]. Xuet al.[11] engineered SFC-YOLOv8
for cold-rolled strip steel inspection, introducing coordinate
attention mechanisms to counter non-uniform industrial illu-
mination. Chenget al.[9] proposed an Enhanced Feature
Extraction Network (EFEN-YOLOv8) utilizing multi-receptive
field convolutions to detect minute structural flaws in metallic
substrates. In the domain of polymer and thin-film casings,
Zhouet al.[15] implemented Large Separable Kernel Attention
=== PAGE 3 ===
3
(LSKA-YOLO) to capture elongated surface scratches, demon-
strating that expanding the effective receptive field along axial
directions substantially aids scratch delineation. Similarly, Yuet
al.[25] incorporated shallow attention convolutions (SAConv)
into packaging inspection networks to retain high-frequency
texture representations frequently lost during deep pooling
layers.
Guptaet al.[21] conducted a comprehensive comparative
benchmark of YOLOv8, YOLOv7, and YOLO-NAS across
multiple automated optical inspection scenarios, establishing
that anchor-free decoupled head architectures deliver superior
gradient stability compared to anchor-based predecessors when
learning irregular flaw topologies. Furthermore, Jianget al.[16]
investigated injection-molded container quality control using
lightweight neural backbones, underscoring that maintaining
high-resolution feature maps in shallow stages is essential for
detecting early-stage thermal oxidation spots.
B. Oriented Bounding Box (OBB) Regression Architectures
The transition from horizontal bounding boxes to arbitrary-
oriented bounding boxes has been extensively explored in
remote sensing and aerial reconnaissance, and has recently
achieved significant traction in industrial metrology [1], [8].
Yanget al.[23] proved that decoupled regression heads capable
of predicting angular offsets eliminate boundary discontinuity
artifacts prevalent in classical angular regression formulations.
Denget al.[13] derived a Gaussian Wasserstein Distance
(GWD) and Probabilistic IoU framework, modeling rotated
rectangles as two-dimensional Gaussian distributions. This
formulation renders the rotated intersection loss continuously
differentiable with respect to both spatial center and rotation
angle.
In industrial manufacturing, Chenet al.[3] investigated
whether oriented bounding boxes enhance small defect detec-
tion on cylindrical bearing end faces, concluding that OBB
models mitigate background noise contamination by over 60%
compared to HBB. Wanget al.[1] deployed RGS-YOLO
with rotated heads for surface crack tracking on wind turbine
blades. Huanget al.[4] proposed MSFE-YOLOv11-OBB
for complex wood grain defect localization, showing that
multiscale feature fusion combined with orientation heads
substantially elevates recall on diagonal fissures. Sunet
al.[7] deployed geometric-aware rotated crack detectors (DS-
YOLO-RC), demonstrating superior resilience against spatial
orientation shifts. Parallel investigations by Liet al.[5] and
Zhanget al.[6] validated YOLOv8-OBB for photovoltaic solar
cell flaw mapping, emphasizing that arbitrary angle prediction
is vital when defect aspect ratios exceed5 : 1.
Zhaoet al.[12] formulated RST-YOLOv8, integrating rotated
spatial transformer networks to normalize irregular polymer
deformation flaws prior to classification. Qianet al.[26]
mathematically resolved boundary discontinuity in oriented
object regression by constructing a continuous modulo mapping
function, eliminating sudden gradient shocks when bounding
boxes oscillate across the−π/2toπ/2boundary.
C. Small-Object and Micro-Defect Localization Challenges
Detecting tiny surface flaws whose pixel footprints occupy
less than 0.1% of the image canvas represents one of the
most demanding frontiers in industrial computer vision [28].
Patelet al.[10] examined automated inspection lines for
pharmaceutical and food blister packs, emphasizing that micro-
pinholes frequently escape detection due to convolutional
stride downsampling. Liuet al.[18] introduced DMD-YOLO,
employing dual-branch deformable convolutions to dynamically
reshape sampling grids around tiny micro-pinholes on food
sealing films.
Linet al.[20] conducted an empirical study on data
augmentation suppression, demonstrating that standard mosaic
augmentation severely disrupts gradient flow on delicate linear
defects. Their findings revealed that downscaling multi-image
mosaics causes spatial anti-aliasing filters to blend faint scratch
pixels directly into the background, a finding that strongly
mirrors the empirical baseline failures observed in our work.
Fenget al.[22] reinforced that high-throughput packaging
container inspection requires maintaining uncompromised
optical resolution throughout feature extraction to reliably
capture sub-millimeter defects.
D. Robotic Optical Sorting and Edge Automation
The integration of deep learning inference engines with
programmable logic controllers (PLCs) and robotic sorting
arms represents a core pillar of Industry 4.0 [27], [29].
Menget al.[14] constructed an automated robotic vision cell
for food containers, demonstrating that high-precision defect
boundary estimation directly determines the grasp success of
robotic rejector effectors. Fenget al.[22] formulated a high-
throughput packaging integrity verification line operating with
rotated convolutions. For edge intelligence, Wanget al.[19]
benchmarked lightweight tensor engines, proving that ONNX
Runtime and TensorRT acceleration reduce YOLOv8 inference
variance on embedded edge processors. Songet al.[17]
demonstrated multi-camera conveyor packaging inspection,
while Sunet al.[30] finalized an edge-deployable OBB setup
for sealing flaw isolation in perishable food casings.
Zhanget al.[24] developed a 6-DoF robotic arm sorting
workcell guided by YOLOv8-OBB pose estimation, confirming
that orientation angles derived from rotated bounding boxes
allow robotic suction grippers to avoid defective surface regions
during grasping. Royet al.[27] designed an automated defect
rejection architecture for rigid packaging containers, validating
deterministic hardware handshake protocols between edge
computer vision systems and high-speed pneumatic diverters.

B. YOLOv8-OBB Neural Network Architecture
The core deep neural architecture is formulated upon
single-stage oriented object detection principles, consisting
of three structurally coordinated stages: the Feature Extraction
Backbone, the Multiscale Neck, and the Decoupled Oriented
Bounding Box Head.
1) Backbone Network:The backbone utilizes cross-stage
partial bottleneck blocks ( C2f ) interconnected with strided
convolutions. Input image tensors I∈R 3×640×640 are pro-
cessed through five progressive hierarchical stages (P1 through
P5 ) yielding downsampling ratios of 4,8,16, and 32. The
C2f module enhances gradient flow by splitting channel
representations and routing them through parallel bottleneck
branches with residual shortcut connections. The terminal stage
incorporates a Spatial Pyramid Pooling Fast (SPPF) module that
aggregates contextual spatial representations across multi-scale
5×5 max-pooling kernels, significantly broadening the model’s
effective receptive field without expanding computational
FLOPs.
2) Multiscale Feature Neck:Feature representations from
stages P3 (stride 8, high spatial resolution), P4 (stride 16),
and P5 (stride 32, high semantic abstraction) are synthesized
via a bidirectional Path Aggregation Network and Feature
Pyramid Network (PAN-FPN). This multiscale feature routing
mechanism transfers fine-grained spatial gradients downwards
to support tiny defect localization (such as 8×8 pixel
pinholes) while simultaneously projecting abstract categorical
representations upwards to assist macro-defect classification
(such as extensive shrivel wrinkles spanning across the casing).
3) Decoupled OBB Head:Traditional horizontal detectors
predict four coordinate offsets per candidate anchor. In contrast,
the decoupled OBB head allocates dedicated, non-interfering
convolutional branches for object classification, box metric di-
mensions, and rotation angle estimation. An oriented bounding
boxBis uniquely defined as:
B= (x c,yc,w,h,θ)(1)
where(x c,yc)denotes the geometric centroid of the oriented
rectangle in image pixel coordinates, w and h represent the
width along the principal axis and height along the orthogonal
secondary axis, and θ∈[−π/2,π/2) is the rotation angle
measured relative to the horizontal coordinate axis.

E. Micro-Defect Augmentation Theory and Hyperparameter
Optimization
Standard deep learning frameworks default to hyperparameter
regimes calibrated for natural scenes (MS COCO dataset). In
industrial defect detection, these standard augmentations cause
catastrophic learning failure.
1) Pathology and Deactivation of Mosaic Augmentation
(mosaic= 0.0 ):Default YOLO pipelines activate mosaic
augmentation, wherein four randomized images are resized
by 50% and stitched into a single composite mosaic canvas.
For microscopic surface defects spanning only 10×10 pixels,
four-image downscaling compresses the defect footprint to
approximately 3×3 pixels. Under bilinear interpolation, defect
=== PAGE 6 ===
6
pixel values blend into surrounding polymer texture, causing the
defect to vanish below optical noise floors. Deactivating mosaic
augmentation preserves the uncompromised spatial resolution
and high-frequency edge gradients required to identify micro-
scratches.
2) Affine Scaling and Translation Constraints:Natural
scene models apply random scaling of ±50% (scale= 0.5 )
and translations of ±20% (translate= 0.2 ). In packaging
inspection, downscaling a 0.2mm pinhole by 50% obliterates
the defect. We constrain scaling to scale= 0.2 and translation
to translate= 0.05 , retaining flaw structural visibility while
preserving invariance to minor conveyor positional drift.
3) AdamW Optimizer with Cosine Annealing:Stochastic
Gradient Descent with Momentum (SGD-M) exhibits severe
trajectory oscillations across high-curvature valleys in OBB
regression landscapes. We deploy the AdamW optimizer ( lr0 =
10−3, decoupled weight decay = 5×10 −4). The learning rate

IV. EXPERIMENTALSETUP ANDCOMPREHENSIVERESULTS
A. Industrial Dataset Specification and Defect Taxonomy
The research benchmark dataset comprises 5,065 high-
resolution casing images acquired from active food preservation
packaging facilities. The corpus is partitioned into a training
partition of 4,674 images and an independent validation
partition of 391 images (containing 1,867 verified ground-truth
defect instances).
Certified industrial quality assurance inspectors manually
annotated each defect instance utilizing arbitrary-oriented
bounding boxes across nine defect taxonomies:
1) Black Dots:Carbonized polymer degradation particles
formed inside extrusion barrels during high-temperature
polymer processing.
2) Defective Foreign Body:Fibrous airborne particulate
matter and foreign materials trapped within the container
substrate.
3) Flange Bad Cutting:Rough, jagged, or scalloped edge
cuts caused by dull trimming guillotines, jeopardizing
lid sealing.
4) Insect:Airborne biological contamination adhering to
the container surface prior to sealing.
5) Intense Scratch:Deep mechanical gouges and fissures
that penetrate into the casing sidewall, causing hermetic
failure.
6) Pinhole:Micro-perforations formed during uneven ther-
mal vacuum drawing, creating direct gas leakage chan-
nels.
7) Scratch (Superficial):Superficial frictional scuffs result-
ing from conveyor guide friction or container-to-container
contact.

C. Conveyor Rotary Encoder Synchronization and Actuation
Jitter
To eliminate physical synchronization drift caused by minor
motor slip or conveyor belt stretch, the inspection workcell
incorporates an incremental optical rotary encoder (Autonics
E50S, 2,048 pulses per revolution) coupled directly to the drive
shaft of the modular conveyor. Encoder pulses are processed
via an industrial high-speed hardware counter card.
When the optical photoelectric sensor detects the leading
edge of a packaging casing, the hardware counter captures the
absolute encoder tick countC trigger. The downstream position
of the pneumatic diverter nozzle corresponds to a constant
distance Drejector = 350mm , equivalent to an encoder pulse
offset ∆C=D rejector/Kcalib, where Kcalib = 0.0488mm/pulse
is the calibrated linear distance per encoder tick. The target
actuation tick countC target is:
Ctarget =C trigger + ∆C(16)
A real-time interrupt compares the active encoder counter
against Ctarget. When Cactive ≥C target, a hardware interrupt
fires the 24V solenoid driver. Under 10,000 continuous test
cycles on a live conveyor, timing jitter was measured at σjitter <
1.15ms , translating to a spatial ejection positioning repeatability
of±0.92mm . This spatial precision ensures that pneumatic
ejection occurs cleanly at the geometric center-of-mass of
the defective casing without disturbing preceding or trailing
non-defective units.
D. Industrial ROS2 Middleware Integration and PLC Hand-
shake
To ensure interoperability with modern distributed industrial
control architectures, the detection pipeline is encapsulated
within a Robot Operating System 2 (ROS2 Humble) node struc-
ture. Camera frame ingestion is executed as an asynchronous
subscriber to an image transport topic. Detections are broadcast
via a custom ROS2 message interface publishing centroid
coordinates (xc,yc), dimensions (w,h) , orientation angle θ,
and classification confidence τ. A dedicated PLC bridge node
translates ROS2 defect messages into discrete optoisolated
24V digital pulses routed to the sorting pneumatic manifold,
establishing a fault-tolerant deterministic communication link
with zero frame dropping under peak manufacturing loads.

B. Polymeric Specular Reflections and Geometric Curvature
Distortions
Thermoformed rice casings possess curved transition radii
along their perimeter flanges and base corners. Curved semi-
gloss plastic surfaces act as convex cylindrical mirrors. Standard
directional LED bar illuminators create localized specular
saturation zones where pixel intensities saturate at 255 across all
RGB channels. When a pinhole or black dot defect falls directly
within a specular glare zone, the underlying visual information
is physically obliterated, preventing detection regardless of
model depth.
While our coaxial diffuse dome illumination mitigated
specular glare across 98% of the casing surface, extreme corner
radii (R <3mm ) occasionally exhibit residual glare strips.
To overcome this limitation in mission-critical environments,
polarizing optical filters or multi-view photometric stereo
imaging represents a vital avenue for future work.
C. Extreme Aspect Ratio Defects and Rotational Ambiguity
For elongated defects such as scratches and trimming
fissures whose aspect ratios exceed 20 : 1 , minute angular
prediction errors (∆θ= 2 ◦) produce substantial lateral centroid
misalignments at the defect endpoints. Given defect length L,
the maximum endpoint displacementδ endpoint is:
δendpoint = L
2·sin(∆θ)(18)
For an extended 150-pixel crack, a 2◦ angular deviation shifts
the boundary box by over 2.6 pixels, which can cause partial
clipping of defect endpoints. Although the Gaussian Wasser-
stein Distance loss substantially dampens angular boundary flips
compared to Smooth L1 angular regression, defects with near-
circular geometry (e.g., small pinholes where w≈h ) exhibit
inherent rotational ambiguity because any angle θ produces an
identical circular Gaussian representation.
D. Edge Computational Constraints and Memory Bottlenecks
In factory deployment, cost constraints often dictate edge
computing hardware equipped with 4 GB to 8 GB of GPU
VRAM (such as the NVIDIA RTX 3050 Laptop GPU utilized
in this work). During experimental development, attempting to
scale input resolution to 1024×1024 with larger backbones
(such as YOLOv8m-OBB) immediately triggered CUDA Out-
of-Memory (OOM) exceptions.
Specifically, computing multiscale feature maps at 1024×
1024 resolution across 185 layers required over 6.1GB VRAM
for a batch size of 2, exceeding available hardware capacity.
While setting the batch size to 1 enabled execution, batch
normalization statistics degraded, destabilizing convergence.
This establishes that for embedded factory edge devices with
≤4GB VRAM, 640×640 resolution paired with YOLOv8s-
OBB and a batch size of 8 represents the optimal operational
frontier for sustained thermal and memory stability.
E. Thermal Drift and Optical Focal Shift in Industrial Plants
Manufacturing shop floors frequently experience substantial
ambient temperature swings ranging from 15◦C during early
morning shifts to 42◦C during continuous summer extrusion
operations. Industrial cameras and high-magnification telecen-
tric lenses are constructed with aluminum barrels possessing
thermal expansion coefficients of approximately 23×10 −6 K−1.
Across a 25◦C thermal gradient, physical lens expansion
induces an optical focal shift ∆f≈9.2µm . Because telecentric
optical systems have narrow depths of field ( DoF≈±1.8mm
at f/4), uncompensated thermal defocusing causes micro-
pinhole boundaries to blur across several camera pixels,
diminishing peak contrast by up to 22%. Implementing active
thermal compensation housings with thermoelectric Peltier
cooling jackets is recommended for manufacturing installations
where ambient temperature gradients exceed±10 ◦C.
F . Subsurface Optical Scattering in Semi-Translucent Polymers
Thermoformed rice casings are typically fabricated from
unpigmented or semi-translucent polypropylene (PP) sheet
stocks. When illuminated by optical inspection lights, incident
photons do not solely reflect off the exterior surface; rather, a
significant portion penetrates into the polymer bulk, undergoing
multiple subsurface optical scattering events before exiting as
diffuse radiance.
This subsurface scattering acts as a spatial low-pass optical
filter, softening the sharp high-frequency edges of tiny surface
defects such as yellow oxidation spots and foreign airborne
fibers embedded shallowly beneath the surface. While the
Varifocal loss and DFL coordinate distribution modeling
effectively adapt to soft boundaries, developing structured
light pattern projection or short-wavelength ultraviolet (UV
365nm ) surface-selective illumination could further sharpen
defect boundary delineation.

H. Asymmetric Economic Penalties: False Rejection vs. Defect
Escape
In high-speed food packaging lines, the economic costs
associated with classification errors are asymmetric. A False
Positive (Type I error) erroneously rejects a conforming
container, incurring a minor scrap material reprocessing cost
of approximately $0.05 per unit. In stark contrast, a False
Negative (Type II error) permits a compromised container with
a micro-pinhole into commercial retail distribution. This allows
air contamination, bacterial spoilage, customer dissatisfaction,
and brand liability that can exceed tens of thousands of dollars
in commercial product recalls.
Consequently, factory quality standards prioritize near-zero
escape rates (Recall >97% ). The baseline model’s low recall
(46.45%) would allow over 53% of defective containers to
enter retail distribution, representing a prohibitive commercial
failure. The proposed optimized model drives recall to 97.33%
while maintaining precision at 97.22%, achieving the required
industrial reliability threshold.

VIII. CONCLUSION
In this investigation, we developed, optimized, and validated
an automated robotic vision system driven by an enhanced
YOLOv8 Oriented Bounding Box (YOLOv8-OBB) architecture
for precision surface flaw inspection in food container manu-
facturing. By replacing conventional horizontal bounding boxes
with five-parameter oriented representations (xc,yc,w,h,θ) ,
the system eliminates background feature dilution and prevents
non-maximum suppression failures on closely clustered micro-
defects.
Importantly, we identified and resolved a widespread augmen-
tation pathology in industrial deep learning: standard mosaic
augmentation and unconstrained affine scaling destroy sub-
millimeter flaw features during training. By eliminating mosaic
transforms, constraining spatial scaling, and deploying an
AdamW optimizer with cosine learning rate decay, the proposed
model elevated overall detection accuracy from a 49.02%
baseline to 97.55% mAP50 across 5,065 production casing
images. Most significantly, detection accuracy on critical micro-
scratches surged from a failing 12.61% baseline to 95.85%.
Integrated with an ONNX-Slim execution engine and ROS2
middleware, the automated inspection cell achieves a deter-
ministic cycle latency of 24.0 ms (41.6 FPS), synchronizing
flawlessly with downstream pneumatic rejection units on
high-speed conveyors operating at 0.8m/s . Future research
will investigate multi-spectral infrared optical imaging and
INT8/FP8 TensorRT edge quantization to further advance
robotic visual quality control in high-speed manufacturing.
